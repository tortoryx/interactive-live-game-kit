# Introduce your version

[简体中文](../zh-CN/SHARING.md) · [Home](../../README.md)

When sharing a game built with this kit, show what a viewer can do. A short clip connecting one message to its result is easier to understand than a list of feature names.

## Show a complete interaction

Keep the input and outcome in the same sequence: a `repair` message restores a wall, or a viewer's avatar general enters the battlefield. Then show one meaningful change you made to the example.

Label local test footage as a local demo. For real broadcast clips, obtain permission or anonymize viewer names and portraits as appropriate.

Use the dedicated game source from the [OBS guide](BILIBILI-AND-OBS.md). Record setup demonstrations separately with sample configuration that contains no credentials.

## Give interested viewers a next step

Explain how viewers participate, what your version changes, and where to try or download it. Put account, AI and extra-asset setup requirements in the download instructions.

One way to credit the starting point is:

> Built from [Interactive Live Game Kit](https://github.com/tortoryx/interactive-live-game-kit). I changed the example into… Viewers participate by…

Adapt that wording to your project. Keep the MIT notice and the required attribution for individual assets; see [licenses](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).

## Share the source

Use the [runtime checks](VALIDATION.md) to confirm that a fresh download starts, then review files using the [data guide](SECURITY.md). Ask someone unfamiliar with the project to follow your README. Any step they cannot infer belongs in the instructions.
